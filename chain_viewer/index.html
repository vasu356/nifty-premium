How this data is stored
End-of-day, not minute dataSource is the NSE F&O bhavcopy: one snapshot per trading day, published after the close. There is no minute-by-minute or tick data in it. Open is the first trade, high and low are the day's range. Close is NSE's official closing price: the volume-weighted average of trades in the last 30 minutes (3:00–3:30 PM), not the final trade, so it can differ from the last traded price by a few points.
One row per contract per dayA contract is one expiry + one strike + call (CE) or put (PE). Every trading day lists every live contract, roughly 1,800 rows a day across 12–21 expiries (weekly, monthly and long-dated).
Fields in each rowopen high low close premium in ₹ · settle NSE's settlement price · volume contracts traded · OI open interest at the close (shown here in contracts). Change and change-in-OI are computed against the same contract's previous day.
Untraded strikesAbout half of all rows had no trades that day. For those, NSE still prints a close: the previous day's close carried forward. They appear here in grey italics and should not be read as real fills.
Time seriesFollowing one contract across days gives its daily history: one point per day, from listing to expiry. Click a premium in the chain to see it.
SpotNifty 50 open, high, low and close for each day come from NSE's daily index file. Strikes below spot are in-the-money for calls; strikes above are in-the-money for puts.
